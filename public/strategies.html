Each strategy is a checklist of pass/fail rules over a company's fundamentals. A company "passes" a strategy when it clears at least the strategy's pass ratio of its rules. Thresholds below are the defaults — every number is adjustable in Settings.

Graham — Defensive Investor value

Benjamin Graham, The Intelligent Investor — buy sound companies at a margin of safety.

  • Adequate size — revenue ≥ $700M.
  • Strong financial condition — current ratio ≥ 2.0; long-term debt under working capital.
  • Earnings stability — positive earnings each of the last 5 years.
  • Earnings growth — ≥ 33% cumulative over 5 years.
  • Moderate price — P/E ≤ 15, P/B ≤ 1.5, and P/E × P/B ≤ 22.5 (the "Graham number").

Passes at 80% of rules met.

Buffett — Quality & Moat quality

Warren Buffett — wonderful businesses that compound at high returns on capital.

  • High, consistent returns — ROE ≥ 15% for 5 straight years.
  • Low leverage — debt / equity ≤ 0.5.
  • Fat margins — net margin ≥ 10%.
  • Durable growth — earnings up ≥ 50% cumulatively over 5 years.
  • Cash generative — positive free cash flow in ≥ 3 of the recent years.

Passes at 80% of rules met.

Piotroski F-Score quality

Joseph Piotroski — a 9-point financial-strength scorecard for separating improving firms from deteriorating ones.

Nine binary signals across profitability (positive net income & operating cash flow, rising ROA, cash flow > earnings), leverage/liquidity (falling long-term debt, rising current ratio, no dilution), and efficiency (rising margin & asset turnover). Passes with a score ≥ 7 / 9.

Greenblatt — Magic Formula value

Joel Greenblatt, The Little Book That Beats the Market — cheap and productive.

  • Earnings yield ≥ 8% (cheap relative to operating earnings).
  • Return on capital ≥ 20% (productive use of capital).

Threshold-adapted here (the original ranks the whole market). Both rules must pass.

Lynch — GARP growth at a reasonable price

Peter Lynch, One Up on Wall Street — fair prices for real growth.

  • PEG ≤ 1.0 (price/earnings not outrunning growth).
  • Earnings growth between 15% and 50% (real, but not a fad).
  • Debt / equity ≤ 0.8.

Passes at 75% of rules met.

Graham Net-Net (NCAV) deep value

Graham's deepest-value screen — buying below liquidation value.

Passes when the market cap is below two-thirds of net current asset value (current assets minus all liabilities). Rare, and usually found in small, unloved names.

Custom criteria your rules

Build your own screen from metric op value rules (e.g. pe_ratio <= 12, roe_pct >= 20) over any of the computed metrics. Save named custom jobs and re-run them from the Jobs tab. Every metric is defined in Definitions.

These checklists summarize well-known public frameworks for educational purposes. Passing a screen is a starting point for research — not investment advice.
